## 2. Batteries, electronics donation/recycling, refrigerant rules

### 2.1 EPA: Used lithium-ion batteries (verified)
Source: https://www.epa.gov/recycle/used-lithium-ion-batteries
- "Lithium-ion batteries and devices containing these batteries should NOT go in household garbage or recycling bins." They "SHOULD be taken to separate recycling or household hazardous waste collection points."
- To prevent fires: "tape battery terminals and/or place lithium-ion batteries in separate plastic bags."
- EPA names **Earth 911** and **Call2Recycle** as two resources for finding a recycler. Damaged batteries: contact the manufacturer.
- **Businesses vs. households:** EPA says most discarded Li-ion batteries "would likely be considered ignitable and reactive hazardous wastes (… D001 and D003)". Household waste is "generally exempt". EPA recommends that businesses manage Li-ion batteries under the federal "universal waste" rules (40 CFR part 273).
- **Local relevance:** the City of Mobile HHW events accept household batteries, including rechargeables (§1.5).

### 2.2 Call2Recycle drop-offs near Mobile
- EPA lists Call2Recycle as a resource (see above). Its locator page exists at https://www.call2recycle.org/locator/ (fetched, HTTP 200). The locator results load dynamically and could not be read.
- A search summary named Home Depot and Lowe's stores in Mobile as participating drop-offs **(secondary; specific store participation not verified)**.
- **Site recommendation:** link to the locator rather than naming stores.

### 2.3 EPA: Electronics donation and recycling (verified)
Sources: https://www.epa.gov/recycle/electronics-donation-and-recycling (page "Last updated on December 19, 2025") · https://www.epa.gov/smm-electronics/certified-electronics-recyclers
- **Before donating or recycling:**
  - Consider upgrading instead of replacing.
  - "Delete all personal information from your electronics."
  - "Remove any batteries from your electronics; they may need to be recycled separately."
  - Check for local facilities.
- **Where to go:** EPA points to Call2Recycle, Earth911 and Greener Gadgets, with a disclaimer that it does not endorse them.
- **Certified recyclers:** "Currently, there are two accredited certification standards in the United States: R2 and e-Stewards."
  - Both "offer a way to assess the environmental, worker health and safety, and security practices of entities managing used electronics."
  - The standards "require destruction of all data on used electronics".
  - Certified recyclers are audited and overseen by "independent accredited certifying" bodies. The ANSI-ASQ National Accreditation Board (ANAB) accredits the certifying bodies.
  - EPA "recommends using certified electronics recyclers" for businesses, governments and large purchasers.
- **Local note:** no R2 or e-Stewards certification was found for Ransom Recycling. **Not verified either way.** Do not describe Ransom or any downstream partner as "certified" without proof.

### 2.4 Alabama e-waste law context (secondary)
- Search summaries say Alabama has **no statewide e-waste landfill ban or producer take-back law**. They also say business-generated e-waste (e.g., CRTs) may need a hazardous-waste determination under ADEM rules.
- **Not verified against primary statute or ADEM text.** Do not make legal claims about Alabama law on the site without checking ADEM directly.

### 2.5 Refrigerant: EPA Section 608 safe-disposal requirements (verified)
Source: https://www.epa.gov/section608/stationary-refrigeration-safe-disposal-requirements

**Equipment dismantled on-site** (e.g., central residential AC, retail refrigeration, chillers) "must have refrigerant recovered in accordance with EPA's requirements for servicing prior to their disposal."

**Equipment that enters the waste stream with its charge intact** (household refrigerators and freezers, window ACs, vehicle ACs):
- "The final person in the disposal chain (such as a scrap metal recycler or landfill owner) is responsible for ensuring that refrigerant is recovered from equipment before its final disposal."
- If an appliance arrives with no charge, the final processor must keep a **signed statement** with "the name and address of the person who recovered the refrigerant, and the date that the refrigerant was recovered". Alternatively, a supplier contract can cover this. The contract option "is not appropriate for use with infrequent suppliers (e.g., individuals and 'peddlers')."
- "EPA does not mandate or accept a sticker as a form of verification".
- "Individuals recovering refrigerant from small appliances, MVACs, and MVAC-like air conditioners, when preparing them for disposal, are not required to be certified technicians." However, recovery equipment "must meet the same performance standards" as servicing equipment.

**What this means for a hauler:**
- Zoiris is not the "final person" if it takes refrigerators or ACs to a scrap recycler or landfill. The facility is responsible for ensuring recovery, and may require evidence of recovery or charge a fee.
- Never vent refrigerant or cut lines.
- **Local data point:** Ransom charges "a $30 fee for any item containing freon" (§4.3). The City will not collect Freon appliances at the curb (§1.3).

### 4.2 Patterns observed
- **Architecture:** the two strongest players (GOT-JUNK, Delta) use a hub plus **item pages** (appliance, TV, mattress, electronics, refrigerator) plus **city pages**. Delta goes further with **city × service** combinations. Smaller locals (BAMA, Big Bite) publish many thin city pages. That is the pattern Google's doorway policy warns about (§3).
- **Pricing:** volume- or truckload-based with a minimum, plus "free on-site estimate", is standard. Few show what is *excluded*. Few explain extra fees for Freon items, CRTs or tires.
- **Trust signals:** star ratings, Google review embeds, "licensed and insured", "same-day".

### 4.4 Content gaps / opportunities
1. **Nobody local explains the City rules clearly.** Examples: no electronics or appliances on the curb pile; TVs are banned from City drop-off bins; Freon appliances are refused; the 2-cubic-yard limit; the contractor-debris rule. This is a genuine, citable help-content gap.
2. **TV disposal is the clearest pain point.** City bins refuse TVs, Clean Sweep refuses CRTs, and Ransom charges for flat screens and is unclear on CRTs. Nobody local has a clear "what happens to your TV" page.
3. **E-waste pages are generic.** Competitors don't name where items go, don't explain data wiping (EPA advice), don't mention battery removal, and don't explain the R2/e-Stewards distinction.
4. **Appliance and refrigerant transparency.** No competitor explains the Section 608 chain of responsibility or why Freon items can cost more.
5. **Commercial e-waste for small businesses.** Ransom only picks up from businesses, and the City drop-off excludes businesses. Residents and small offices that can't haul items themselves are underserved.
6. **Unincorporated areas.** Residents of Theodore, Tillmans Corner, Semmes and others can't use City drop-offs (residency rule). This is a real reason to need pickup.

## 8. Implications for the Zoiris site

### 8.1 Safe to claim (once the business confirms each item)
- Zoiris picks up junk, furniture, mattresses, appliances and electronics in Mobile and nearby Mobile County communities. List only the areas actually served. All ten places in §5.1 are real and in Mobile County.
- Electronics are taken to appropriate recycling outlets. **Name the actual outlet** (e.g., Ransom Recycling) only if that is true and the outlet agrees.
- Zoiris handles items the City won't take at the curb or in drop-off bins (TVs, appliances, electronics), for a fee. Explain *why*, citing City rules.
- Phone 251-930-8621.

### 8.2 Do NOT claim (unless documented)
- **"Certified," "R2," "e-Stewards," "EPA-certified," or "certified data destruction"**, unless Zoiris or its named downstream recycler holds the certificate. Link to proof if so.
- **"We wipe your data"** or "guaranteed data destruction" without a defined process. Safer: follow EPA advice and tell customers to delete personal data first. Note that Ransom offers destruction "upon request".
- **Freon / refrigerant recovery by Zoiris**, unless properly equipped. Accurate framing: refrigerant appliances go to a facility that handles recovery under EPA Section 608, and a surcharge may apply.
- **Hazardous waste pickup** (paint, chemicals, propane, fluids). Send customers to the City HHW events instead.
- **Star ratings, review counts, "#1," "best in Mobile," "licensed and insured," "same-day," "24/7,"** unless true and verifiable. Do not reuse the cleaning site's review numbers.
- **A street address you don't staff.** Avoid placeholder addresses like "123 Dauphin St". Google requires real, staffed locations, and service-area businesses should hide their address.
- **Specific City fees or Ransom fees as guarantees.** Link to the sources and say "as of [date]".

### 8.3 Site-structure recommendations (aligned with Google policy)
- **Avoid doorway/scaled patterns.** Don't build dozens of near-identical city or neighborhood pages (the "480+ locations" approach). Use **one service-area page** listing the communities actually served, with genuinely different information where it exists. Example: "Theodore and Tillmans Corner are unincorporated, so City of Mobile drop-off centers aren't available to residents."
- **Item / service pages with real substance:**
  - TV & electronics pickup
  - Appliance & refrigerator removal (refrigerant explanation)
  - Furniture & mattress removal
  - Cleanouts (estate, rental, garage)
  - Small-business e-waste pickup
- **A cited, original "Mobile disposal guide" hub.** Cover the City drop-offs and the TV exclusion, the curb rules, the 2-cubic-yard limit, the contractor-debris rule, HHW event info, Operation Clean Sweep, battery safety (EPA) and data-wipe advice (EPA). Include "last checked" dates and links. This fills the biggest competitor gap and is the kind of people-first content Google's helpful-content guidance describes.
- **Show FAQs on-page but don't expect rich results.** The FAQ rich result is gone as of May 7, 2026. Write FAQs for users and AI features; extra FAQPage markup gives no SERP benefit.
- **Structured data:** use `LocalBusiness` (or a subtype) with real `name` and `telephone`. Google lists `address` as required. If Zoiris has no public address, don't invent one. Accept that the rich-result eligibility may be limited, and keep markup consistent with the GBP. Don't add self-serving `aggregateRating`.
- **No special "AI optimization" files are needed** (no llms.txt for Google). Focus on indexable, accurate text and a complete Google Business Profile.
